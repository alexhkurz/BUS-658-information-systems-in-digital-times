## Learning Objectives
By the end of this course, students will be able to:
- Understand the mathematical foundations of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and digital platforms, including neural networks, attention, online communication protocols, and distributed consensus.
- Generate insightful research and discussion questions using Large Language Models (LLMs) and other research tools.
- Analyse the impact of AI and digital platforms on the economy, business, and society, using macroeconomic theory and empirical evidence.
- Work collaboratively in interdisciplinary teams to statistically analyse empirical questions about, or propose innovative solutions to, challenges around AI and digital technologies.
- Critically evaluate the future implications of AI and digital platforms, including ethical, social, and regulatory aspects.


## Grading and Assignments

- **20% Participation**
  - One of the co-teachers will monitor class discussion participation quality.
  - Put in a daily grade for each category (good / mediocre / zero): 0 / 1 / 2.
- **20% Student discussion questions**
  - The day before class, students submit approximately 1–2 questions based on the scheduled reading.
  - These will be graded rigorously based on the knowledge students will have access to via LLMs and the internet (and students are encouraged to use them!) when generating the questions.
  - Open question: should students also include tentative answers?
- **15% Reflection 1**
  - At the end of week 4, we will suggest to each student a topic for reflection based on one of their proposed questions.
  - Students will have two weeks to complete a 500–1,000 word reflection on this topic.
  - Open question: encourage or require an analytical element?
- **15% Reflection 2**
  - At the end of week 10, we will suggest a second reflection topic based on students' proposed questions.
  - Students will have two weeks to complete a 500–1,000 word reflection on this topic.
- **30% Final Project**
  - The final project asks students to imagine how AI and digital technologies could (or should) be used in the future.
  - Possible forms include (but are not limited to):
    - A novel statistical analysis, based on public or collected data, meant to answer an empirical question about the effect of technology on some outcome.
    - A computer program/demoing a digital platform feature they hope to emerge.
    - A film visualising the user experience for a proposed or speculative AI or digital technology, and investigating its consequences.
    - A business plan and investment pitch for a profitable and socially beneficial enterprise using these technologies.
  - We will encourage students to form interdisciplinary teams to work on these projects (although they may work individually).
  - Grading takes team size into account, but grading rigor scales sub-linearly with group size to encourage groups.
